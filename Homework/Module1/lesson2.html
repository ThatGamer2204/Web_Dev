Mini Biography: Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam was an Indian aerospace scientist and statesman who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007.

Early Life

Born in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, he grew up in a humble family and sold newspapers as a child to support his education. Despite limited resources, his curiosity about flight and mathematics drove him to study physics and aerospace engineering.

Important Achievement

He played a leading role in developing India's first indigenous Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III), which successfully deployed the Rohini satellite in near-Earth orbit. Known worldwide as the Missile Man of India, he was admired for his humility and lifelong dedication to teaching. (Note: He was awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honor, in 1997.)

Many people assume he was only a politician a dedicated scientist and educator at heart.

Even complex aerospace engineering relies on basic scientific principles, from understanding fuel compounds like H2O to calculating speeds beyond 102 meters per second.

Words That Inspire

Dr. Kalam often encouraged students to aim high, famously reminding them to dream, dream, dream because dreams transform into thoughts and thoughts result in action.

"If you want to shine like a sun, first burn like a sun."
